# Short procedural submissions — preferred visual reference

Use this reference for a short legal or administrative *escrito*, especially a one-page *subsanación*, *aportación documental*, or short procedural request. It records the approved **visual pattern**, not any specific person's facts, the authority's compulsory rules, or a template that must be filled mechanically. If a filing portal or tribunal prescribes another format, follow that instead.

## Page composition

1. **Top, centred:** the authority's formal name, bold uppercase, then its unit or division, centred and regular-weight on the next line. No logo, sender's address, salutation, or decorative masthead.
2. **Left-aligned identification block:** one or two short lines with the expediente and the reference of the notice or proceeding. The primary label may be bold; avoid a table, boxes, or oversized headings.
3. **Centred title:** a specific uppercase bold title such as `ESCRITO DE APORTACIÓN DOCUMENTAL Y SUBSANACIÓN`, on its own line with noticeably greater spacing before the body than between ordinary paragraphs.
4. **Opening paragraph:** the filer's name and capacity (and whom they represent, if applicable), case link, and a conventional procedural verb such as `EXPONE:`. Use bold selectively for the name, not throughout the identity paragraph.
5. **Exposition:** two or three justified paragraphs explaining the notice, the document supplied, and its relevance. Keep natural, full-sentence prose without numbered facts unless a rule or complex facts warrant them.
6. **Petition:** a short bold `Por lo expuesto, SOLICITA:` followed by one justified paragraph identifying precisely what the authority should do; do not turn one coherent request into a numbered shopping list.
7. **Enclosure:** a single plain line or short paragraph naming the attached document(s), where applicable.
8. **Closing:** a discreet place-and-date or electronic-submission formula, often aligned right, with a signature only if required.

## Schematic (generic placeholders, not real case data)

The following shows hierarchy and reading order, not mandatory legal wording or literal whitespace:

```text
            A LA [AUTORIDAD COMPETENTE]
                  [Unidad o división]

Expediente: [identificador]
Referencia del requerimiento: [identificador]

        ESCRITO DE APORTACIÓN DOCUMENTAL Y SUBSANACIÓN

[NOMBRE Y APELLIDOS], en calidad de [condición procesal],
en el expediente indicado, comparece y EXPONE:

[Primer párrafo: qué se requirió o qué se comunica y en qué fecha.]

[Segundo párrafo: documento aportado, su objeto y alcance.]

Por lo expuesto, SOLICITA:

[Petición concreta en prosa completa y sin numerar.]

Documento que se acompaña: [descripción breve].

                  En [lugar], en la fecha de su presentación electrónica.
```

## Practical rendering guidance

- A4 portrait, black on white, Times New Roman, usually 11–12 pt body; the title and authority can use the same or a modestly stronger size. Do not use oversized display type.
- Conventional margins (for example approximately 2.5 cm) and justified body paragraphs. Add moderate line spacing and clear *inter-block* separation; keep consecutive paragraphs moderately close. Avoid excessive vertical gaps or crushed text.
- Centre only the addressee, division, and title; left-align case identifiers; justify substantive paragraphs; right-align the final filing-place/date formula where that convention is appropriate.
- Selectively bold the authority, case label, specific title, opening identity, and petition heading. Keep the rest ordinary weight.
- No numbering merely for its own sake; no headings such as `HECHOS`, `FUNDAMENTOS DE DERECHO`, or `ANEXOS` when they do not serve this short document.
- No running header. Usually no visible footer on a one-page submission; if the document spans multiple pages, any page-number footer contains only a centred numeral.
- Never create extra text to fill the page, hide missing information by inventing it, or insert private names, ID numbers, or case references from a screenshot used as a style example.
- Inspect the final rendered PDF/DOCX, not just the source: check spacing, alignment, readability, paragraph justification, orphaned lines, and page count.

For longer submissions, comprehensive legal analysis, or complex pleadings, retain the main skill's restrained multi-section approach instead of forcing this compact layout.
